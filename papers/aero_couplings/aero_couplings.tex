\title{Four ways to couple a vortex-step aerodynamic model to a flexible kite:\\
a comparison on the TU~Delft V3}
\author{SymbolicAWEModels.jl contributors}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A flexible kite is simulated by coupling a structural model with many degrees of
freedom to an aerodynamic model that is too expensive to solve at every evaluation of
the equations of motion. SymbolicAWEModels.jl offers four ways to make that coupling
with the Vortex Step Method (VSM): \code{AeroDirect} holds the solved panel forces
constant between solves; \code{ContinuousAero} holds only the induced velocity and
re-evaluates every panel force from the live wing state; \code{AeroPressure} does the
same and places each panel force on the structure through the airfoil pressure
distribution; and \code{AeroPressure} with live polars also regenerates each panel's
polar and pressure distribution from its deformed shape with NeuralFoil. The couplings
suit different structural models, so we fly the TU~Delft V3 kite, built with V3Kite.jl,
as two: a particle system under \code{AeroDirect}, \code{ContinuousAero} and live
polars, and a new dynamic model of Timoshenko beam elements under \code{AeroPressure}.
All cases fly the same steering manoeuvre at the same flight condition, and we compare
their stability, cost, load placement and sensitivity to deformation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The leading-edge inflatable (LEI) kites used for airborne wind energy deform
substantially in flight: the canopy billows, the struts bend and the whole wing twists
when it is steered through its bridle. An aero-structural model of such a kite pairs a
structural model of point masses, spring segments and, in this paper, beams, with an
aerodynamic model that turns the deformed shape and the apparent wind into
loads. A lifting-line model with 2D section polars, the Vortex Step Method
(VSM)~\citep{cayon2023vsm,vortexstepmethod}, is accurate enough for a curved LEI wing
and cheap enough to run inside a dynamic simulation~\citep{poland2023}, but solving its
nonlinear circulation problem at every evaluation of the structural right-hand side
(RHS) is still far too expensive for an implicit time integrator that evaluates the RHS
and its Jacobian many times per step.

The coupling therefore always freezes \emph{something} between VSM solves. What is
frozen decides three properties of the simulation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item whether the aerodynamic forces respond to the wing's motion between solves,
    which is what supplies aerodynamic damping to fast structural modes;
  \item where on the structure each panel force is applied;
  \item which deformations of the wing the section polars can see.
\end{enumerate}
SymbolicAWEModels.jl~\citep{symbolicawemodels} implements four couplings that make
different choices, selected per wing with the \code{aero\_mode} argument of
\code{load\_sys\_struct\_from\_yaml}. This paper describes them in a common notation,
flies each on the same kite and manoeuvre, and states what each one is for.

\section{Aero-structural model}

\subsection{Two structural models of the V3 kite}

The TU~Delft V3 is a \SI{25}{m^2} LEI kite with a well documented geometry.
V3Kite.jl~\citep{v3kite} models it in two ways that share the bridle, the kite
control unit (KCU) and the tether: a network of spring segments and pulleys down to the
KCU and a braked winch holding \SI{250}{m} of tether. They differ in the wing.

\paragraph{Particle model.} (\code{system\_psm.yaml}) The wing is ten pairs of point
masses, one at the leading and one at the trailing edge of each of ten structural
stations, joined by stiff spring-damper segments along the leading edge, the struts
and the trailing edge. A station is a chord line and nothing more: the model has no
point between the leading and the trailing edge, so it can twist, bend and change its
span, but its sections keep their airfoil. The aerodynamic geometry has
\NumSectionsParticle{} sections with polars from CFD, refined into
\NumPanelsParticle{} VSM panels.

\paragraph{Beam model.} (\code{system\_beam.yaml}) The leading edge and the struts
are inflated Timoshenko beams, joined at the same ten stations, each of which also
carries eleven chordwise control points on the canopy, held by the struts and by
canopy segments. The sections can therefore deform chordwise, which is what the
pressure placement of \code{AeroPressure} acts on and what live polars read. To our
knowledge it is the first dynamic kite model that resolves the inflatable frame in beam
elements; its structural formulation and validation are the subject of a separate
paper. Its aerodynamic geometry has \NumSectionsBeam{} sections sliced from the V3
surface mesh, with NeuralFoil~\citep{neuralfoil} polars and surface pressure tables
for every section, refined into \NumPanelsBeam{} VSM panels.

\subsection{Common aerodynamic core}

Every coupling starts from the same VSM solve. Given the panel geometry and the
apparent wind $\vect{v}_{a,i}$ at each panel $i$, the solver finds the bound
circulation $\Gamma_i$ that makes the lift of each panel consistent with its section
polar at its effective angle of attack. From the converged solution follow the induced
velocity $\vect{v}_{\mathrm{ind},i}$ at each panel's control point and the panel force
\begin{equation}
  \vect{F}_i = q_i\, c_i\, b_i \left( C_{L}(\alpha_i)\,\hat{\vect{l}}_i
             + C_{D}(\alpha_i)\,\hat{\vect{d}}_i \right),
  \qquad
  q_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \left| \vect{v}_{a,i} + \vect{v}_{\mathrm{ind},i} \right|^2 ,
  \label{eq:panel-force}
\end{equation}
with chord $c_i$, span $b_i$, and lift and drag directions $\hat{\vect{l}}_i$,
$\hat{\vect{d}}_i$ normal and parallel to the effective inflow. The angle of attack
$\alpha_i$ is measured in the panel's own chord frame, which is reconstructed from the
structural points that bound it. A VSM solve runs every \code{vsm\_interval} time steps
of the structural integrator; the couplings differ in what they keep from it.

\paragraph{AeroDirect.}
The panel forces of the last solve are distributed to the structure and held constant
until the next one,
\begin{equation}
  \vect{F}_j(t) = \vect{F}_j(t_k), \qquad t_k \le t < t_{k+1},
\end{equation}
a zero-order hold. Each panel's force and moment are split between the leading- and
trailing-edge points of its section such that both are preserved. The RHS reads the
point forces as parameters, so the aerodynamics adds nothing to the cost of an RHS
evaluation or to the Jacobian. It is the simplest coupling and the only one that
supports every VSM wing type, but it is a loosely coupled, explicit partitioned
scheme~\citep{farhat2006}: within a step the structure moves under forces that do not
know it moved, and the structural Jacobian contains no aerodynamic damping.

\paragraph{ContinuousAero.}
Only the circulation solve is frozen. At each solve the induced velocity of every
refined panel is stored, and every RHS evaluation rebuilds each panel's chord frame from
the live structural points, forms the inflow
$\vect{v}_{a,i}(\vect{x},\dot{\vect{x}}) + \vect{v}_{\mathrm{ind},i}$, and evaluates
eq.~\eqref{eq:panel-force} symbolically with the section polars at the resulting
$\alpha_i$. The force on each panel is scattered onto its two bounding sections as
$0.75\,\vect{F}_i + \vect{C}_i$ at the leading edge and $0.25\,\vect{F}_i - \vect{C}_i$
at the trailing edge, where the couple $\vect{C}_i$ carries the pitching moment. Because
$\partial\vect{F}/\partial\dot{\vect{x}}$ now enters the Jacobian, the implicit
integrator sees the aerodynamic damping of every mode that changes the angle of
attack, and a stale induced velocity costs far less than a stale force: the induced
velocity changes with the circulation distribution, which varies slowly compared with
the local inflow.

\paragraph{AeroPressure.}
The panel force is evaluated exactly as in \code{ContinuousAero}, but it is placed on
the structure through the airfoil's surface traction. At each solve the section's
pressure and skin-friction tables are evaluated at the panel's angle of attack, and each
contour node $k$ receives the traction
\begin{equation}
  \vect{t}_k = \left( -C_{p,k}\,\hat{\vect{n}}_k + c_{f,k}\,\hat{\vect{s}}_k \right)
               q\, A_k ,
\end{equation}
which is lofted onto the nearest structural points of the panel's station. Between
solves the traction pattern is frozen, and each point receives its frozen traction plus
an equal share of the difference between the live panel force and the frozen pattern's
net force, so the point forces always sum to the live total. VSM owns the magnitude,
the pressure distribution owns the placement. On a wing whose stations carry chordwise
points, such as the beam V3, this is the only coupling that loads the canopy between
the leading and trailing edge.

\paragraph{AeroPressure with live polars.}
The tabulated polars of the three couplings above belong to the undeformed sections, or
to a family of sections indexed by a single flap angle. With live polars every
station's chordwise control points are read as a deformation of its airfoil: their
offsets from the deformed chord line become an increment of the section's Kulfan
(CST) parameters~\citep{kulfan2008}, NeuralFoil is evaluated on a grid of angles of
attack around each panel's current $\alpha$, and the result becomes the panel's polar
for the next solve. After the solve, the pressure distribution is regenerated from the
same deformed shape. The polars therefore follow any chordwise deformation the
structure can express, not only a known flap or chord-fraction deformation, at the
price of a neural-network evaluation per solve whose accuracy is only as good as
NeuralFoil's for the deformed shape.

\section{Test case}

\paragraph{Which coupling on which model.} A coupling is only as good as the structure
it loads. \code{AeroPressure} exists to place the load between the leading and the
trailing edge; on the particle model there is nothing there, and its pressure scatter
reduces to the leading/trailing-edge split of the other couplings. It is flown on the
beam model. \code{AeroDirect} and \code{ContinuousAero} place their loads on the
chord ends and are flown on the particle model, the structure they were written for.
Live polars are flown on the particle model as well. There they see the angle of attack
and the Reynolds number of each panel but no chordwise deformation, since a particle
station has no point off its chord line; the polars they fit are NeuralFoil's for the
undeformed sections, not the tabulated ones. What they add on a deforming section is
shown on the beam model in Section~\ref{sec:sections}. Because \code{AeroPressure}
needs surface pressure tables, the live-polar cases fly the particle model on the
NeuralFoil geometry of the beam model rather than on its CFD polars.

\paragraph{Manoeuvre.} Both models start from their own settled state at
\SI{250}{m} tether length, \SI{60}{\degree} elevation, \SI{15.4}{m/s} of uniform wind
and \SI{20}{\percent} depower, with the winch braked. The steering is raised
open-loop by \num{\Steering} above its settled value, ramped linearly from
\SI{\RampStart}{s} to \SI{\RampEnd}{s} and then held, which turns the kite out of the
zenith into a loop through the power zone for the rest of the \SI{\SimTime}{s}. An
open-loop input gives every coupling the same excitation; a heading controller would
feed each coupling's own response back into it.

\paragraph{Integration.} The structure is integrated with the FBDF solver of V3Kite's
settings at a fixed output step $\Delta t$ of \SI{50}{ms} or \SI{10}{ms}, with a VSM
solve every step. \code{AeroDirect} is also flown at \SI{2}{ms}, and
\code{ContinuousAero} at \SI{10}{ms} with a solve every fifth step. A case stops
early when the integrator fails, one output step takes more than \num{1000} solver
steps, the wing's geometry becomes invalid for the VSM, or it has run for
\SI{20}{min} of wall time. The wall times are single-threaded, measured by the
simulation's own step timers on one core of a shared machine, and are comparable
between the cases only to within some ten percent.

\subsection{Stability and cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cases} lists every case. Only one did not finish:
\code{AeroDirect} at \SI{50}{ms} folded the particle wing within its first second,
until its aerodynamic sections crossed over and the VSM geometry became invalid. At
\SI{10}{ms} and \SI{2}{ms} it flies the whole manoeuvre, and so do
\code{ContinuousAero}, live polars and \code{AeroPressure} at every step they were
given. This is the pattern the loosely coupled scheme predicts: under a zero-order
hold the structure moves for a whole output step under forces that do not see it
move, and the energy that feedback would have removed is left to grow in the fast
modes of the wing. The couplings that evaluate the panel forces from the live state
are stable at the coarse step.

The cost is set by the step each coupling needs. \code{ContinuousAero} at
\SI{50}{ms} is the cheapest case in the table, about half the cost of
\code{AeroDirect} at the \SI{10}{ms} it needs, because it needs five times fewer VSM
solves. Its symbolic force evaluation costs integration time: at the same
\SI{10}{ms} step it spends about twice as long integrating as \code{AeroDirect}. A
solve every fifth step moves the cost from the VSM to the integrator rather than
removing it, and doubles the high-frequency content of the angle of attack and
nearly quadruples that of the wing-node speed: an induced velocity five steps old is
felt. Live polars cost a NeuralFoil evaluation per panel and solve, which dominates
their cost at both steps.
\code{AeroPressure} on the beam model is the most expensive coupling, mostly because
the beam model has five times the points of the particle model and a denser Jacobian;
its VSM share is comparable to that of \code{ContinuousAero}.

\subsection{Flight}

Figures~\ref{fig:tracking} and~\ref{fig:trajectory} show the manoeuvre. On the
particle model \code{AeroDirect} and \code{ContinuousAero} fly the same loop to within
the line width: once both are stable, the hold on the forces changes nothing at the
scale of the flight path. Live polars fly a tighter loop at a lower tether force. They
see no deformation on this model, so the difference is the polar itself: NeuralFoil's
polar of each undeformed section against the CFD polar the other two couplings use.
The beam model under \code{AeroPressure} turns faster, flies a tighter loop and pulls
more, at a lower angle of attack; the two models differ in structure, mass and polars,
so this is a difference between the models rather than between couplings.

Figure~\ref{fig:station-loads} shows where each coupling puts the load. On the
particle model the whole section force lands on the two chord ends. On the beam model
\code{AeroPressure} spreads it over every point of the station, following the
tabulated pressure distribution whose suction peak sits just behind the leading edge,
so the canopy between the struts is loaded as well. With live polars the same station
carries a larger load, spread further aft.

The reason is in Figures~\ref{fig:polar} and~\ref{fig:pressure}. After one second of
the manoeuvre the canopy of the beam model has billowed. The tabulated polar belongs
to the undeformed section and knows nothing of it; the live polar is NeuralFoil's for
the section as it now is. Its lift at the operating point is more than twice the
tabulated one, at a lower angle of attack, with a nose-down moment four times larger,
and its suction peak has moved from the leading edge to a third of the chord. That is
the case live polars are for. It is also what makes them fragile on the beam model:
the circulation solve over polars refitted to strongly billowed sections does not
always converge, and in the first second of the manoeuvre \num{7} of the \num{100}
solves of the live-polar section case did not.

\section{Discussion}

Each coupling does what it is for, on the model it is for.

\code{AeroDirect} is the simplest: the aerodynamics adds nothing to the RHS, works with
every wing type, and is the reference the others are checked against. Its price is
stability. With no aerodynamic damping in the Jacobian it needs a step short enough
that the structure cannot move much between solves, \SI{10}{ms} on the particle V3,
and a VSM solve at every one of those steps.

\code{ContinuousAero} is the efficient choice on a particle model. The panel forces
follow the wing between solves, so the implicit integrator sees the aerodynamic
damping of the fast modes and stays stable at a five times larger step, which is what
halves its cost against \code{AeroDirect} although its RHS is dearer. Solving the VSM
less often than every step is possible, but the stale induced velocity shows up as
high-frequency content in the flight.

\code{AeroPressure} is for a structure with points between the leading and trailing
edge. There it puts the load where the pressure acts, so the canopy and the struts are
loaded as they are in flight, which is what a model that resolves them needs. On a
particle model it has nothing to act on.

Live polars are for sections that deform in ways a polar table cannot anticipate. A
table indexed by one flap angle covers a known chord-fraction deformation; live
polars cover any shape the structure can express, as the billowed beam section
shows. They pay for it with a NeuralFoil evaluation per panel and solve, with
NeuralFoil's accuracy on shapes far from its training set, and with circulation solves
that stop converging when the deformation grows. On the particle model they are
stable but see no deformation, so there they only replace the tabulated polar with
NeuralFoil's.

Two limits apply to what is shown here. The particle and the beam models differ in
structure, mass, polars and settled state, so a comparison across them is one of
models, not of couplings; within each model the cases share everything but the
coupling and the step. And the manoeuvre is a single open-loop turn at one flight
condition; a reel-out, a gust or a depower step would load the fast modes differently.

\section{Reproducing the results}

The paper lives in \code{papers/aero\_couplings} of the SymbolicAWEModels.jl
repository, with three scripts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{make\_aero\_geometry.jl} slices the V3 mesh and writes the NeuralFoil
    section tables;
  \item \code{run\_simulations.jl} flies the cases and logs them;
  \item \code{make\_figures.jl} writes the figures and the numbers quoted here.
\end{itemize}
The section figures use \code{plot\_station\_loads!}, \code{plot\_panel\_polar!} and
\code{plot\_panel\_pressure!} of the package's Makie extension.
